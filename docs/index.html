The phenomenon

Same question.
Same answer.

An answer is written once and frozen, byte for byte. A scorer model rates it from 0 to 10 and explains why.

Add one sentence.

Before scoring, the scorer reads a single line claiming the answer's author once gave its answer a 2/10. Nothing else changes.

The score drops.
The reason stays silent.

The same correct answer falls from 8 to 4. The explanation finds a "mistake" that isn't there and never mentions the history it was given.

What we found

        
A small sentence, a consistent shift.

        
Every scorer, every dataset and 94% of tasks move the same way. A low rating of the scorer's own answer lowers the score most, but unrelated low ratings and purely verbal negativity also pull scores down, so this is contextual influence rather than a uniquely personal grudge.

      Transplant layer 24. The score follows.

On 72 held-out tasks, it moves about a fifth of the way in both directions. The same transplant at layer 2 does nothing.

      i
      
        
      Scope. The mechanistic study is exploratory and covers Llama only. A single averaged steering direction did not reliably reproduce the effect (CI includes zero) and did not beat a random direction, which points to a causally relevant but task-dependent representation rather than a single switch.
      
      
      
    
      

    
    
      
      
      
        
      Why it matters
      
        
      Explanations are not an audit trail.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      01
      Models judge models
      LLMs grade answers, rank alternatives and give feedback to other agents. Their judgments carry context from earlier interactions.
      
        
      02
      Reading the reasoning misses it
      When the explanation stays silent about what moved the score, explanation-based oversight can't see the dependence.
      
        
      03
      Audit with counterfactuals
      Swap the context, hold everything else fixed and compare. That catches what the explanation leaves out.
